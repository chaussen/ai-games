WOBBLE WORKS 搭把手

A team of small, wobbly characters with two independently controlled hands share one job: deliver a single, absurdly heavy Glow Core to a beacon. Nobody owns a piece of the puzzle. You push, lift, hang, swing, stack, link hands and occasionally fling each other — and the level never tells you which of those was the "right" answer.

Designed as the higher-challenge sibling of the one-button rhythm relay: same room, same zero-install LAN host, but the difficulty now lives in the world and the physics, not in a timing window.

01 The brief & the answer

"A team of players just act like normal game characters together to complete tasks. Depending on complexity, it can range from simple scripted puzzles, to an open world map to achieve something in creative ways." Reference: Human: Fall Flat.

What we took from the reference

  • Embodied avatars, not abstract buttons. A character has weight, momentum, two hands, and can be used as a tool.
  • Physics is the puzzle. HFF began as a controls prototype; playtesting with the creator's son showed he ignored the puzzles and just played with the physics engine, so the game was rebuilt around that (Wikipedia).
  • Countless routes. "Your only goal is to get to the exit at the other end of the level. So it's all about finding different routes to it" (GamingBolt interview).
  • Shared experience. "Co-op in Human: Fall Flat is for better and for worse an entirely shared experience" (same interview); some puzzles need a second player (Xbox Wire, GDC 2016).
  • Failure is the joke. Slapstick converts repeated, unfair failure into fun (IGN review).

What we changed for a family on a sofa

  • One shared object. The Core is too heavy for one wobbler. Interdependence is enforced by physics, not by a rule or a score penalty — measured at ~16 px/s solo vs ~56 px/s with three players in the prototype.
  • Phone-sized controls. Four verbs only: left, right, jump, and two hold-to-grab hands. HFF's independent arm control (Fanatical interview) survives as two buttons.
  • Asymmetric screens. One TV shows the whole workshop; each phone gets a personal follow camera plus quick-call pings. Everyone can see the shared goal; nobody has to squint at a TV from the couch.
  • No dead ends. Poof-respawn at a soft checkpoint, no lives, no score, no elimination. The Core is indestructible and never loses progress.
  • Collectible routes, not a leaderboard. Each chapter ends with a playful Route Medal and a stamp in a persistent Family Scrapbook.
Design hypothesis, not a proven finding: cheap failure keeps a mixed-age group playing. The research supports that slapstick makes repeated failure enjoyable and that respawn cost measurably shapes experience (Die-r Consequences, IEEE CoG 2021), but we found no source proving "no-lives keeps groups playing". We treat it as a deliberate design bet and say so.

Why "one more round" works here: a 30–90 s chapter, a visible shared object, a surprise medal each time, and a scrapbook with empty slots. There is no score to optimise, so replaying is about how, not how fast.

07 Mechanics & rules

Four verbs, five bodies

  • ◀ ▶ move — velocity-controlled with momentum preserved through collisions.
  • JUMP — a short hop; while a hand is attached, jump becomes a climb assist.
  • L GRAB / R GRAB — hold to attach that hand to the nearest grabbable body within ~58 px; release to let go. Works on crates, planks, barrels, rope nodes, static ledges, the Core, and other wobblers.
  • Two hands on one object = carry; two players on one plank = shared carry; a line of players holding hands = a human chain.

Weight, not rules

  • The Core is a heavy rolling circle. A tuned weight-transfer model gives each wobbler leaning into it a share of drive — 1 player ~16 px/s, 3 players ~56 px/s, 4 demo bots ~80 px/s in the prototype.
  • It applies only while grounded, so catapult flights, slopes, wind zones and falls keep full Matter.js physics.
  • See-saws are real pivot constraints; ropes are real constraint chains; plates, gates, water and wind are real sensors/regions.

Failure & progress

  • Fall in water or off the map → poof, respawn at the last soft checkpoint in under a second. No lives, no score, no elimination.
  • The Core is indestructible; if it falls in water it returns to the checkpoint it last passed, never to the start.
  • Nothing is timed out. A round ends only when the Core reaches the beacon.

Rewards

  • Route Medal derived from mechanics actually used: HUMAN CHAIN, CATAPULT CREW, BRIDGE BUILDERS, THE ACROBATS, TWO-HAND CARRY, or CHAOS GREMLINS.
  • A stamp joins a persistent Family Scrapbook (chapter, medal, time, poofs). No numeric leaderboard.
  • Quick-call pings ("HERE / MINE / PUSH / LOL") appear above the sender on all screens — a low-friction reason to talk.

Why this netcode

  • Server-authoritative physics: nobody can desync a shared rigid-body world.
  • 60 Hz fixed simulation, 20 Hz compact snapshots (positions + angles + flags), client-side exponential smoothing. No extrapolation — Gaffer notes it fails for non-linear rigid-body motion.
  • LAN RTT is typically 1–3 ms, so client-side prediction / reconciliation / lag compensation are unnecessary. They are the roadmap item if this ever goes online.
  • Deterministic lockstep was rejected: it needs bit-level determinism and stalls on a phone Wi-Fi spike.

Constraints we designed around

  • Offline: Matter.js and the QR generator are vendored. No CDN, no npm install, no account.
  • Join: /config.js injects the host's real LAN IPs so the QR never encodes "localhost" when the board is opened on the PC.
  • AP / client isolation: guest Wi-Fi can block phone↔PC traffic entirely. The README tells you to use the main SSID.
  • Secure contexts: a plain http LAN origin is not trustworthy, so clipboard and other privileged APIs may be unavailable — hence the copy fallback and plain ws:// (no mixed content on an http page).
  • Discovery: no mDNS promise; browser mDNS is a WebRTC IP-privacy mechanism, not a LAN service-discovery API. A QR/short link is the join mechanism.

09 Prototype status

Implemented & verified

  • Zero-install Node host; vendored Matter.js 0.20.0 + vendored qrcode-generator.
  • Authoritative 60 Hz physics; 20 Hz snapshots; board + phone clients; keyboard and touch controls.
  • Two-hand grabbing on any body, ropes, see-saws, plates, gates, water, wind, soft checkpoints, tool dispenser, bots, pings, Route Medals, persistent scrapbook.
  • All 5 spaces (4 chapters + sandbox) with real geometry as drawn above.
  • Automated smoke test: board + 3 phone controllers + 2 demo bots complete CH.1, press the plate, open the gate, light the beacon and write a stamp.
  • Headless Chromium checks: zero console errors, zero failed HTTP responses on both board and phone.

Deliberately out of scope (roadmap)

  • Client-side prediction + server reconciliation (only needed for internet play).
  • 3D rendering / full ragdoll joints — this is a 2D side-view proof of the mechanics.
  • A real level editor with saved placements beyond the sandbox dispenser.
  • Auto-generated "best blooper" replay, local TLS for secure-context APIs, mDNS hostname.
  • Accessibility pass: colour-blind palette, audio-only cues, one-hand control scheme.
  • Balance pass with an actual family — bot data is not human playtest data.

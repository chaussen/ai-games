## Addendum — discoverability fix (fixes R4-D012's BOUNCE)

Your Part D session (John, unprompted) found the pipeline itself flawless but the compose gesture undiscoverable: a filled inventory chip was a static 15px circle, no different-looking from an empty one, so nothing ever invited a grab-and-drag. Fix, affordance-only (no text, no tutorial, still out of M0 scope):

- **A chip that just received matter pops 3x, then breathes** — a slow continuous pulse for as long as it's holding something. Works without hover, so it reads on touch too. Pauses while you're actively dragging it (the flying glyph is the live visual then) and picks back up if the drag snaps back instead of consuming the chip.
- **Mouse hover brightens a filled chip's ring** — additive on top of the pulse, mouse-only.
- **The drag-time overlay now also marks empty ground.** Occupied cells still get the 4-zone compose box; an empty walkable cell now shows a soft "place here" ring while you're carrying something over it. Dropping there already freed the inventory slot — now it's visible that it does, which is the direct fix for "can't swap or give up."

Re-verified against the live build (headless Chromium + CDP): chip visibly pulses at rest immediately after harvest with zero drag input; hover brightening confirmed; the drop ring appears over empty ground and clears on release with the slot correctly freed; idle pulse resumes after a snap-back release. Full S1–S9 suite below re-run clean on top of this change, zero console errors, zero regressions.

**This is ready for you to re-run Part D.** Nothing else changed — same room, same recipes, same zone geometry from the original M0.1 pass below.

## Addendum 2 — placement is now adjacent-only (R4-D016)

Follow-up feedback on the discoverability fix: once the chip became visible/draggable, it turned out you could drop it **anywhere on the board** — drag a carried component clear across the room and compose two cells that had nothing to do with each other. That's telekinesis, the same summoning failure the charter already rules out, just one step removed.

Fixed: a compose or place drop now only resolves if the target cell is **orthogonally adjacent to the avatar** (reach 1, no diagonals). Carrying still works exactly the same — components travel with you in the satchel — but the loop is now strictly **pick up → walk → compose**. Drop on a far cell and it just snaps back, same as dropping on a wall.

One thing to watch for in this Part D run specifically: adjacent-only makes retrying a fizzle slightly more expensive (walk back to re-grab, walk back to re-try). We don't have data on whether that reads as friction yet — your last session never got far enough to attempt a single composition. If it feels laborious this time, flag it — the fix on deck is a small one (decompose drops components at your feet instead of nearby), not a reason to loosen the adjacency rule.

## Addendum 3 — reach visibility + click/drag fix (R4-D017)

Two more rounds of feedback on the adjacency change:

1. **"I can only drag to four squares, and I like the chip's highlight — show something similar on cells I can't reach, because I still want to try them."** Fixed: the moment a drag starts, the (up to) 4 cells you can actually reach get a soft persistent border, visible for the whole drag rather than discovered one hover at a time. And a cell you *can't* reach now shows a dimmed X instead of nothing — before, trying a far cell just did nothing, which reads as broken rather than "not in range."

2. **"Clicking a collected character can drop it directly in front — that's inconsistent with dragging, and it might feel like chips only need a click."** This was a real bug: a plain click always has a pixel or two of pointer jitter, and the drag system's default sensitivity was 0px — so a click alone could register as a tiny drag and silently place/compose wherever it landed. Fixed by requiring 8px of movement before a drag is recognized at all. A plain click on a chip now does nothing; only a deliberate drag acts.

Re-verified against the live build: a zero-movement click on a filled chip changes nothing; a real drag still shows the reach highlight and composes correctly; hovering a far cell shows the blocked X while the reach highlight stays visible; releasing there snaps back cleanly. Full regression suite re-run clean, zero console errors.

## Addendum 4 — chip/board click overlap (R4-D018)

Last one: **"clicking the middle inventory chip sometimes moves the avatar up one cell — it's meant to do nothing on click."** Real bug, not perception. The chips float above the avatar in the world (not a screen panel), and the middle one sits so close to the cell boundary above it that clicking near the top of its circle was landing in that cell instead — and the game's walk-on-tap logic had no idea a chip was in the way, so it just walked you there.

Fixed: a click is now checked against the chips first, before it's ever treated as a board tap. If it's on a chip, it's a chip interaction (or nothing, per Addendum 3's threshold) — never a walk.

Verified against the live build by clicking the middle chip at 9 points across its entire clickable area, including the exact spot that used to leak — avatar never moved, inventory never changed, in any of them. Regular taps elsewhere on the board still walk the avatar normally. Full regression suite clean, zero console errors.

## What changed

**1. Zone picker rebalance + drag overlay (fixes R4-D008)**
- The invisible "which slot am I dropping into" zone was buggy — dead centers and an x-checked-first bug meant releases didn't always go where they looked like they'd go. Fixed: y is checked before x. `top`/`bottom` are full-width bands (top 33% / bottom 33% of the cell); the middle 34% splits into `left`/`right` at the halfway line. Every pixel in the cell now resolves to one of those four — there is no dead zone and no `enclose` anymore (that's deferred to M1, when 囗 exists to use it).
- While you're carrying a component and hover over a cell that has something in it, the cell now shows its own 4-zone subdivision (faint lines) with the zone you're currently pointing at lit up (colored fill, tinted to the thing you're carrying). It's drawn right on the cell, not in a side panel, and disappears the instant you let go or move off. On touch, the lit zone fills all the way to the cell's outer edge so it's still visible even with a fingertip sitting in the middle of the cell.

**2. 末 (mò, "treetop/tip") wired in (fixes R4-D009)**
- Drop 上 (the "up" mark) on the **top** zone of a bare 木 → it locks into 末, glows, pulses, and says "mò" — same treatment as 林/炎/沐.
- Visual effect: the tree grows one cell taller. If the cell directly above is empty, a 末 glyph fades and rises into it. If that cell is occupied (by another matter, a wall, or the avatar), the lock still happens but the growth is suppressed — no crash, no weirdness, just no visible growth that time.
- Every other place you can drop 上 (onto 林, 森, 水, 火, a stone, or another 末) fizzles, same as before. That's intentional — 上 now has exactly one thing it locks into, and several interesting near-misses.

**3. 口 removed from this build (fixes R4-D007 flag)**
- The vent (口's only source in the room) is gone. 口 isn't harvestable, isn't on the board, doesn't react to anything. Live material set for this playtest is **水 火 木 上**, plus 石 as inert scenery.
- It's not deleted from the code, just commented out — it comes back at M1 alongside 鸟/鸣 where it'll actually have something to do.

**4. Luminosity numbers cleaned up (fixes nothing visible — internal only)**
- 火's and 炎's "how much darkness they clear" values used to be scattered inline in the reaction code. They're now in one place. Behavior is identical to what you already played — this was a code-hygiene fix, not a balance change.
